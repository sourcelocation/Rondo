A **note** holds what you write. Its **type** decides which **cards** you study. One note can make several cards, each with its own schedule.

## Add a note

Open a deck and choose **Add notes**. You write on the card itself: the fields sit where they show when you study, under **Front** and **Back**. Choose the type above the card to switch it; the picker shows each type as a small card. Fill in the fields, then choose **Add** (or <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>Enter</kbd>); the editor stays open for the next note, and **Undo** takes back the one just added. If a note can't make a card yet, Rondo says what's missing, and when it makes several, **See them** shows each.

To edit a note, choose it in a deck or in [Browse](/search), or press <kbd>E</kbd> while studying: the editor opens over the card, and the session carries on after you save.

## Note types

| Type               | Use it for                                                            |
| ------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Basic              | One question, one answer.                                             |
| Basic and reversed | A card in each direction.                                             |
| Type the answer    | Typing the answer; case and accents don't count.                      |
| Cloze              | Hiding parts of a text: one card per cloze number.                    |
| Image occlusion    | Hiding parts of an image: drag on it to draw masks, one card each.    |
| Vocabulary         | A word, its reading, meaning and sound: recognize it, then recall it. |

### Clozes

Select some text and choose **Cloze** (<kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>⇧</kbd> <kbd>C</kbd>). Each new cloze gets the next number; text with the same number hides together on one card. Put the cursor inside a cloze to give it a hint, shown in the blank, or to show its words again.

## Formatting

Select text and its formatting appears above it: bold, italic, underline, highlight, clozes, code, subscript, superscript, maths, readings, lists and tables. You can also type maths straight into the text: `$x^2$` becomes inline maths, a line of `$$\int f$$` a block.

A **reading** is ruby text over a word, for furigana or pinyin: select the word and choose **Reading**.

Drop, paste or choose an image; it's kept at most 2,048 pixels wide. Sounds can be dropped, chosen from a file or recorded. **Replace** and **Remove** change them.

## Your own types

In the type picker, choose **Add custom type** and a type to start from, or open **Settings › General › Note types**. Your types have a pencil to change them. A type is shaped on the card:

- **Fields** sit on its front (what it asks) or its back (the answer): text, an image or a sound. Rename a field where it is, move it up or down, or add one to either side.
- A text field can be **in the deck's language**: Rondo reads it aloud in that language's voice and spell-checks it in that language. Other fields are never read aloud. In the built-in types, Front (and a vocabulary note's Word and Example) is in the deck's language; give the deck a language in its settings.
- **Questions** add cards: **Ask both ways** also asks from the answer, **Type the answer** asks you to type it first, **Listening** plays a sound on its own.

**Every card, block by block** shows everything else: each card's front and back as blocks (a field, a divider, a label, or a type-in box), field sizes, and **One card per cloze or mask**. The preview shows the first card for sample text as you go.

<Callout title="Keep prompts focused" type="idea">
  Ask for one fact at a time. Put explanations or examples on the answer side so the prompt stays easy to recall.
</Callout>
